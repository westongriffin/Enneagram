Overview The 9 Types Mislabelings Take a Test

Take a Test

Both tests ask you to rate statements from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Answer for how you've been across your whole adult life, not just lately — and answer for who you are, not who you'd like to be. Alongside the type items, both tests include screening items for patterns that famously imitate enneagram types (ADHD, anxiety, depression, autism, trauma, OCD/OCPD, social anxiety). If your answers fit one of those look-alike patterns and it overlaps your top types, your result will carry a ⚑ flag explaining the possible mislabeling.

Quick · ≈ 5 minutes

Quick test

A first hypothesis with a coarse look-alike check. Two formats: rate statements (24 items, agree–disagree) or multiple choice (14 scenario questions where you pick the answer that fits best — good if you tend to agree with everything).

Exhaustive · ≈ 15–20 minutes

Exhaustive test

Full look-alike screening with onset and context questions that behavior-only tests skip. Two formats: rate statements (75 items) or multiple choice (30 scenario and either/or questions, including items that put a type and its clinical look-alike side by side).

Before you start: this is a self-reflection tool. It cannot diagnose any condition, and its mislabeling flags are hypotheses, not findings. If you're currently in a depressive episode, an anxiety spike, or fresh trauma, your answers will reflect the state more than the trait — the honest move is to retake it when the storm has passed, and to bring the state itself to a professional.
